**Summary:**

- Waiting line (queuing) systems consist of customer arrivals, a service system, and departures, with key features including queue discipline, arrival and service processes, and customer behaviors like balking and reneging.
- Kendall-Lee notation (A/B/k) classifies queuing systems by arrival distribution (A), service distribution (B), and number of servers (k), with common distributions being Markovian (M), Deterministic (D), and General (G).
- The M/M/1 queue model assumes Poisson arrivals, exponential service times, a single server, infinite queue length, and FCFS discipline, with key performance metrics derived using Little’s Law and utilization factor ρ.
- The M/G/1 and M/D/1 models extend the single-server queue to general and deterministic service time distributions respectively, affecting queue length and waiting time calculations.
- Queue performance depends heavily on utilization; as utilization approaches 1, queue length tends to infinity, highlighting the importance of balancing arrival and service rates.
