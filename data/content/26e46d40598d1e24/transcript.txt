Decision Tree
•A graphical representation of a decision problem 
according to the logical sequence in their time of 
occurrence for events
•Consists of Decision, Event, and Payoff nodes
•Drawn in a left-to-right manner indicating events 
unfolding from early to later stages.
•Able to display multi-stage decision problem by 
listing exhaustively all possible decision and event 
paths.
8

Notation and Symbol
Decision event: square 
Chance (uncertain) event: circle
Value (payoff): rounded rectangle
Link: pointed arrow or arc
9
•Use a square for a decision event and a circle for a chance 
event
•Use a link leaving a chance event for a different outcome 
(state of nature), and a link leaving a decision node for a 
different decision alternative.
•Display the payoff at the end of each limb of a tree (the 
result for the series of branches that make up that limb).

Evaluating A Decision Tree
•Start from the “end branches” and work 
leftwards towards the “root”
•For each chance node, calculate the EV 
and write the EV above the node
•For each decision node, select and mark 
the decision with the maximum EV.  Write 
the optimal EV above the decision node
•The optimal sequence of decisions is given by 
the route marked. The optimal EV is given 
above the “root” node

Revision of Probabilities
Now suppose that you have hired a market research 
company to conduct a research.  The research results 
indicate that the market acceptance level will be high. 
Will the probability for the market acceptance level to 
be actually high (or moderate, or low) with this 
forecast or will it still be the same as before?
14

The Bayes Rule
1.Prior belief
2.New information such as forecast, new research 
findings, experts’ opinions, etc.
3.The prior belief is revised in the light of the new 
information
4.The updating depend on the prior probabilities 
and the reliability of the new information source
