LECTURE MEMORY:  
- Decision analysis involves making choices under uncertainty, with or without probabilities.  
- Decision criteria include maximizing maximum payoff (optimistic), maximizing minimum payoff (pessimistic), minimizing regret, and maximizing expected value.  
- Payoff tables summarize outcomes for decisions under different states of nature.  
- Optimistic (maximax) and pessimistic (maximin) criteria guide decision-making based on best and worst payoffs.  
- Minimax regret criterion minimizes the maximum regret, calculated from a regret table.  
- Expected value (EV) calculation uses probabilities of states to find weighted average payoffs for decisions.  
- Decision trees graphically represent sequential decisions and chance events, showing all possible paths and payoffs.  
- Decision nodes are squares, chance nodes are circles, and payoffs are rectangles in decision trees.  
- Evaluate decision trees by working backward from end nodes, calculating EVs at chance nodes and choosing max EV at decision nodes.  
- Bayes theorem updates prior probabilities based on new information (e.g., market research results) and reliability of the information source.  
- Reliability matrix shows conditional probabilities of research outcomes given true states of nature.  
- Posterior probabilities are computed using Bayes theorem to revise beliefs after receiving new information.  
- Expected Value with Perfect Information (EVwPI) is the weighted sum of the best payoffs for each state.  
- Expected Value of Perfect Information (EVPI) = EVwPI – EV without perfect info; measures value of knowing outcomes in advance.  
- Expected Value of Sampled Information (EVSI) = EV with sample info – EV without info; measures value of imperfect information.  
- Efficiency of information = EVSI / EVPI, indicating how valuable the sample information is relative to perfect info.  
- Optimal strategy depends on cost of information: if survey cost > EVSI, do not conduct survey; else conduct survey and act based on survey outcome.  

Formulas/Theorems:  
- Expected Value (EV) = Σ [Payoff × Probability]  
- Bayes Theorem:  
  P(S_j | I_k) = [P(I_k | S_j) × P(S_j)] / Σ_t [P(I_k | S_t) × P(S_t)]  
- EVwPI = Σ_j [max payoff for state S_j × P(S_j)]  
- EVPI = EVwPI – EV (without perfect info)  
- EVSI = EV (with sample info) – EV (without info)  
- Efficiency = EVSI / EVPI  

Pitfalls:  
- Ignoring the reliability of new information when updating probabilities.  
- Confusing decision nodes and chance nodes in decision trees.  
- Not working backward properly when evaluating decision trees.  
- Overlooking the cost of information when deciding whether to conduct research.  
- Using incorrect probabilities or failing to revise probabilities after new information.  

Example Problem Types:  
- Constructing and analyzing payoff tables using different decision criteria (maximax, maximin, minimax regret).  
- Calculating expected values for decisions given state probabilities.  
- Drawing and evaluating decision trees with multiple stages of decisions and chance events.  
- Applying Bayes theorem to update probabilities after receiving new market research data.  
- Computing EVPI, EVSI, and deciding on the value of conducting research based on survey cost.  
- Determining optimal strategies based on updated probabilities and expected payoffs.
