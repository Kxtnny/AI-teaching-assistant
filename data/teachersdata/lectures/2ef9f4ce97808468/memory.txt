LECTURE MEMORY:  
- Workshop 4 focuses on Literature Review and Search Techniques.  
- Attendance requires at least 65 minutes logged in via NTU email on Zoom.  
- A quiz will be available after the session, with a deadline about one week later.  
- Active participation is expected during the session.  
- Learning outcomes:  
  - Apply the literature review process effectively.  
  - Use library research tools to find scholarly resources.  
  - Identify general and influential papers using citation databases (e.g., Scopus).  
- Research is an inquiry starting from what you already know.  
- Define clearly what you intend to work on or investigate.  
- Identify ideal sources for information: journal articles, databases, experiments.  
- Ensure your research direction is appropriate and focused.  
- The research cycle has five stages:  
  1. Problem discovery and definition (includes literature review)  
  2. Research design  
  3. Data collection  
  4. Data analysis  
  5. Communicating and presenting results  
- Literature review is a major task in the first stage of the research cycle.  
- Before literature review, identify your research question or topic clearly.  
- Citation databases like Scopus help find influential and relevant papers.  
- Use library tools to access scholarly articles and resources efficiently.

Pitfalls:  
- Not attending the required minimum time may affect assessment.  
- Not completing the quiz by the deadline.  
- Starting literature review without a clear research question.  
- Using inappropriate or unreliable sources for literature review.  
- Ignoring the research cycle stages and jumping ahead without proper foundation.  

Example Problem Types:

- Using Scopus to find influential papers on a given topic.  
- Identifying appropriate sources for a specific research inquiry.  
- Mapping your current research stage within the research cycle.
